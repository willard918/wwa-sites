# Can I build an ADU on my lot in Medford?

Often yes. Three things decide it, and only the first is settled by the state.

## 1. The state says the city has to allow one

Oregon law (SB 1051, 2017) requires cities over 2,500 residents to allow at least one accessory dwelling unit with each detached single-family home inside the urban growth boundary, subject to reasonable local siting and design rules. Cities may not require the owner to live on the property or require off-street parking for the ADU (HB 2001, 2019). Medford is well above the population threshold. [verify: current text of ORS 197.312 and Medford's adopted ADU standards]

Sources: [DLCD guidance on SB 1051](https://planning.org/knowledgebase/resource/9139610); [League of Oregon Cities summary of DLCD guidance](https://orcities.org/resources/communications/bulletin/dlcd-updates-guidance-adu-requirements).

## 2. The city decides the details

Size caps, setbacks, height, design standards, utility connection fees and permit steps are set by the city, and they change. Our lot check assumes typical setbacks (front 20 ft, sides and rear 5 ft) so it can tell you quickly whether a unit is even plausible; it is not a zoning determination. ## 3. The land has to cooperate

This is the part a zoning map does not show.

- **Room.** A 24 x 16 ft unit (384 sq ft) needs more than its footprint: setbacks, a clear gap from the existing house, and space to bring a truck and a crane in. We use 6 ft from existing buildings in the lot check.
- **Slope.** We read public lidar elevation data for every lot. A prefab unit wants gentle ground. Where the average slope under the unit is more than about 15%, or the route in from the street is steeper than that, we do not suggest a unit, however much space there is on paper. Some lots in the hills east and south of town are like this. A survey or a design made for the slope can still work.
- **What is already there.** Existing buildings come from satellite-derived footprints, which can be out of date. Easements, flood and fire zones and utilities are not in public data we use, so a survey is always the next step.

## What to do next

1. Put your address into the [lot check](/#top). It shows your lot, the contour lines, where a unit could go and how much room is left.
2. If a unit fits, look at the [configurator](/#configure) and send us the configuration for a written quote.
